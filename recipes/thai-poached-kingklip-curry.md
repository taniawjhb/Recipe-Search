## Ingredients

- 700 grams firm white fish - kingklip, hake or sea bass (thicker cuts are best)
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 lime, zest and juice

### Coconut Thai Curry Sauce

- 2-3 tablespoons neutral oil
- 1 small red onion, finely diced
- 4-6 garlic cloves
- 1-2 fresh hot chillies, finely diced
- 3 tablespoons lemongrass, very finely chopped
- 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
- 1 teaspoon fresh ginger, grated
- 1-3 tablespoons Thai red curry paste (optional)
- 500 millilitres chicken or fish stock
- 8 makrut lime leaves
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 cup carrots, diced (or substitute potatoes)
- 1 red bell pepper, diced
- 1 tin coconut milk
- 1 tablespoon fish sauce
- Squeeze of lime
- 200 grams green beans (or asparagus, snap peas or snow peas)
- 1/2 cup chopped basil (Thai basil is great), cilantro or scallions

## Method

1. Cut the fish into 5 centimetre chunks, pat dry and place in a bowl. Season with salt, lime zest and 1 tablespoon lime juice. Toss to coat and set aside.
2. In a large sauté pan, heat the oil over medium. Add the onion and cook until golden, about 4 minutes. Add the garlic, lemongrass and chillies; sauté 3-4 minutes until fragrant. Reduce the heat to low.
3. Grate in the ginger and turmeric, add the Thai curry paste (if using), and sauté 1 minute. Add the chicken stock, makrut lime leaves, salt, carrots and bell pepper. Cover and simmer gently for 5 minutes, until the carrots are tender.
4. Stir in the coconut milk, fish sauce and a squeeze of lime juice (start with half a lime). Taste and adjust: the broth should be salty, lime-forward and lightly spicy. Add more fish sauce or curry paste as needed; the flavours will mellow once the fish and vegetables are added.
5. Nestle the fish and quick-cooking vegetables into the broth. Simmer gently over medium-low, spooning broth over the fish or turning once the bottom half becomes opaque, about 3-4 minutes. (Alternatively, finish in a warm 175°C oven.)
6. Garnish with basil, Thai basil or cilantro, and serve with lime wedges.
