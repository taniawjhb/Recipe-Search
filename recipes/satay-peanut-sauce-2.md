## Ingredients

- 500g roasted peanuts
- 25g dried chillies, rehydrated in hot water and chopped (blended paste)
- 1 onion, chopped (blended paste)
- 4-5 shallots, chopped (blended paste)
- 5 cloves garlic (blended paste)
- 1-inch ginger, chopped (blended paste)
- 1-inch galangal, chopped (blended paste)
- 2 stalks lemongrass, bruised and sliced (blended paste)
- 1 tsp shrimp paste (blended paste)
- 1/4 cup water, for easier blending (blended paste)
- 1 cup vegetable oil, with extra on the side (peanut sauce)
- 4 cups water, with extra on the side (peanut sauce)
- 100g palm sugar, or to taste (peanut sauce)
- 1 tbsp salt, or to taste (peanut sauce)
- 1 cup coconut milk (peanut sauce)
- 2 tbsps tamarind paste, or to taste (peanut sauce)
- 1 tbsp dark sweet soy sauce (peanut sauce)

## Method

1. Add peanuts to a blender or food processor. Give them a rough pulse, so that you get chunks of peanuts in the sauce. Once processed, remove and set to one side.
2. Blending the aromatic paste: Soak the dried chillies with hot water for 5 minutes, or until the chillies rehydrate and soften. Once rehydrated, drain and chop the dried chillies to smaller pieces for easier blending.
3. Peel and roughly chop the onions and shallots, garlic, ginger and galangal.
4. Remove the hard outer layer of lemongrass, and the top and bottom root part. Bruise the lemongrass with the back of the knife, or a pestle, to release fragrance. Slice at a diagonal.
5. Add all of the prepped ingredients in the blender. Add about 1/4 cup of water for easier blending. Turn the blender on and blitz to a smooth paste.
6. Making the peanut sauce: Add plenty of oil to the wok, and heat over low to medium heat. Once hot, add the blended paste.
7. Continuously stir-fry until the paste is fragrant and slightly dries down. The paste should have emulsified and released oil. If the paste is too dry before oil is released, add more oil. This 'pecah minyak' or oil-splitting process can take 10 minutes or so. Once the dried chilli paste has cooked down, add the blitzed peanuts and water. Stir to combine.
8. Add your blocks of palm sugar and salt to taste. Let this come to a boil, before turning the heat down and allowing to simmer for 15 minutes, or until the palm sugar blocks melt.
9. Once the palm sugar has melted, add coconut milk and tamarind paste. If too much liquid has cooked off, add more water. Add dark sweet soy sauce.
10. Let the sauce come up to another boil. Taste test and season accordingly with sugar and salt, if needed. Once you're happy with the taste, the satay sauce is done. Serve hot!
