## Ingredients

For the pâte sucrée:

- 220 grams plain/all purpose flour
- 50 grams soft icing sugar / powdered sugar, sifted
- 15 grams almond meal / ground almonds
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- 100 grams (7 tablespoons) unsalted butter, softened, cut into 1 centimetre cubes
- 1 large egg, at room temperature

For the curd filling:

- 2 tablespoons lemon zest (2 lemons' worth)
- 250 millilitres lemon juice (from 1-2 lemons)
- 300 grams white sugar
- 340 grams unsalted butter, cut in 1 centimetre (1/2 inch) cubes
- 6 whole large eggs
- 6 egg yolks (from large-size eggs)

## Method

1. Place all the dry ingredients in the bowl of a food processor.
2. Add the cold butter and pulse until the butter is incorporated. Now add the whole egg and run the machine until the dough starts to come together.
3. Gather the dough and wrap in clingwrap, shape into a 2 centimetre thick disc, place in the freezer for 10 minutes.
4. Remove the pastry from the freezer and on a lightly floured surface roll out until 3 millimetres thick.
5. Transfer the pastry to your baking tin and gently ease it into the sides, do not stretch the pastry as this will cause shrinking. Dock the pastry thoroughly to avoid the pastry from rising. Place the pastry shell in the freezer for 10 minutes.
6. Remove from the freezer, add a sheet of baking paper and fill with baking beans, place in the preheated oven to 200°C / 390°F (180°C fan). Bake for 15 minutes then remove the beans and baking paper and bake for a further 10 minutes until the base is lightly golden. Remove from the oven and allow to cool completely before filling, this will keep the crust nice and crisp.
7. For the filling, preheat the oven to 180°C. Put all the filling ingredients in a medium saucepan and whisk to combine.
8. Place the saucepan on the stove over low / medium-low heat. Whisk constantly, especially as the butter is melting, to ensure it does not split. Keep stirring until the mixture thickens enough to visibly mound (i.e. holds its shape briefly) on the surface when dolloped - about 5 minutes, though it might take longer depending on stove strength and saucepan heat retention.
9. Strain into a bowl using a fine mesh strainer.
10. Pour into the tart shell and smooth the filling surface using an offset spatula or similar.
11. Bake for 5 minutes. It will still be a soft custard when you touch it but not liquid. It will set more when cooled so it is sliceable.
12. Allow to cool completely before cutting as this will allow the tart to firm up.
