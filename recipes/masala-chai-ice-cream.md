## Ingredients

- 1 cup (250 millilitres) full cream milk
- 2 black tea tea bags
- 4 large egg yolks
- 1/2 cup (120 grams) castor sugar
- 6 whole cardamom pods
- 12 whole cloves
- 3 whole star anise
- 1/2 teaspoon whole black peppercorns
- 3 whole cinnamon sticks
- 1/2 inch (1.5 centimetres) fresh ginger root
- 2 cups (500 millilitres) thickened cream
- 1 tablespoon mild flavoured honey, such as clover

## Method

1. Add milk and cream to a pan on the stove top over medium heat. Add all of your spices and the tea bags; if you want, bruise the spices first in a mortar and pestle. Once warm but not boiling, turn off the stove and leave the milk mixture to cool. Leave the tea bags to steep for three to four minutes, then remove them.
2. In a bowl, add the egg yolks and sugar and beat until pale and creamy.
3. Add the warm spiced milk and cream mix to the eggs about 1/3 at a time, mixing well between additions, to temper the egg yolks.
4. Return the spiced egg custard mixture to a pot, add the ginger and honey, then place the pot on a low heat and start to cook the custard. Be very careful to prevent the mix heating too much and scrambling the eggs.
5. Heat on a low to medium heat, watching carefully and stirring well to prevent lumps. The mix is done when it coats the back of the spoon or spatula and leaves a clear line when you run your finger across it.
6. Remove from the heat and allow to cool for a few minutes. Transfer the mix to a heatproof container and chill in the fridge for at least four hours, preferably overnight.
7. Strain the ice cream to remove any whole spices and ginger pieces, then place in the bowl of your ice cream machine and churn to the manufacturer's instructions. It will be the consistency of soft serve when done. Transfer to a container and store in the freezer until fully set.
